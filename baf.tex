\section{Theoretical Foundations of UIDT}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{The Information-Density Field and Action Principle}
\label{subsec:field}

The foundational postulate of the Unified Information-Density Theory asserts that the information density field $S(x)$ constitutes the primary ontological entity from which all physical phenomena emerge. This field is a Lorentz-covariant scalar with mass dimension $[S] = M^1$ in natural units where $\hbar = c = 1$, carrying the physical dimensions of entropy per unit volume: $[S] = [k_B \ell^{-3}]$, where $k_B$ denotes Boltzmann's constant and $\ell$ represents a characteristic length scale. The explicit dimensional structure ensures that $S(x)$ represents a local information content measurable in fundamental units of bits per cubic meter when appropriately normalized by $\ln 2$.

The dynamics governing the information density field derive from a covariant action principle. The field satisfies a generalized Klein--Gordon equation modified by non-minimal couplings to both gravity and Yang--Mills gauge fields. The complete action takes the form:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:action}
\mathcal{S} = \int \mathrm{d}^4x \sqrt{-g} \left[ \frac{1}{2\gamma_{\mathrm{UIDT}} \ell_{\mathrm{Pl}}^2} g^{\mu\nu} \nabla_\mu S \nabla_\nu S - V(S, \rho_m) + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}} \right],
\end{equation}
where $g$ represents the determinant of the spacetime metric $g_{\mu\nu}$, ensuring diffeomorphism invariance as required by general covariance. The Planck length $\ell_{\mathrm{Pl}} = \sqrt{\hbar G/c^3} \approx 1.616 \times 10^{-35}~\mathrm{m}$ establishes the fundamental scale where quantum gravitational effects become dominant. The dimensionless coupling constant $\gamma_{\mathrm{UIDT}}$ appears as a prefactor to the kinetic term, effectively rescaling the canonical normalization of the scalar field and serving as the universal information-to-energy conversion factor. The covariant derivative $\nabla_\mu$ ensures that the kinetic term respects both diffeomorphism invariance and local Lorentz symmetry, with the connection coefficients accounting for the curvature of spacetime.

The potential $V(S, \rho_m)$ governs both self-interactions of the information density field and its response to local matter distributions $\rho_m$. This potential is constrained by thermodynamic consistency requirements and by the demand that the theory reproduce established physics in appropriate limits:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:potential}
V(S, \rho_m) = \frac{1}{2} m_S^2 S^2 + \frac{\lambda_S}{4} S^4 + f(\rho_m) S,
\end{equation}
where the mass parameter $m_S$ determines the Compton wavelength $\lambda_C = \hbar/(m_S c)$ controlling the range over which information density correlations persist. The quartic self-coupling $\lambda_S$ ensures the potential remains bounded from below, preventing runaway instabilities that would render the vacuum unstable. The matter coupling function $f(\rho_m)$ makes the theory responsive to local mass-energy distributions, providing a mechanism for environmental (“chameleon-like”) screening effects that explain why information density variations remain undetectable in terrestrial laboratory experiments despite their cosmological significance.

The interaction Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}$ encodes the crucial non-perturbative coupling between the information density field and Yang--Mills gauge theory. For an $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ gauge group relevant to quantum chromodynamics, this takes the explicit form:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:interaction}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}} = -\frac{\kappa}{\Lambda} S(x)\, \mathrm{Tr}\!\left(F^{\mu\nu} F_{\mu\nu}\right),
\end{equation}
where $F_{\mu\nu}$ represents the Yang--Mills field strength tensor, the trace runs over Lie algebra generators in the adjoint representation, $\kappa$ is a dimensionless coupling constant, and $\Lambda$ carries dimensions of mass, representing an ultraviolet cutoff scale. This interaction term couples the information density field directly to the topological charge density of the Yang--Mills vacuum, modifying the vacuum structure of quantum chromodynamics and generating an effective mass gap for gluonic excitations. The form of this coupling is not arbitrary but follows necessarily from requiring that the combined system admit a non-trivial renormalization group fixed point ensuring asymptotic safety.

The coupling to gravity enters through the Einstein--Hilbert action augmented by the stress-energy tensor derived from the information density field:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gravity}
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{grav}} = \int \mathrm{d}^4x \sqrt{-g} \left[ \frac{c^4}{16\pi G} R + \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{matter}} + \mathcal{L}_S \right],
\end{equation}
where $R$ denotes the Ricci scalar curvature. The stress-energy tensor for the $S$ field is obtained through functional differentiation with respect to the metric:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stress_energy}
T_{\mu\nu}^{(S)} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \frac{\delta \left( \sqrt{-g}\, \mathcal{L}_S \right)}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.
\end{equation}
This yields the canonical form for a scalar field stress-energy tensor, but with modified coefficients arising from the non-minimal coupling through $\gamma_{\mathrm{UIDT}}$. The effective pressure $p_S$ and energy density $\rho_S$ associated with the information field are related through the equation of state parameter $w_S = p_S/\rho_S$, which evolves dynamically as the universe expands, providing the mechanism for dynamical dark energy.
