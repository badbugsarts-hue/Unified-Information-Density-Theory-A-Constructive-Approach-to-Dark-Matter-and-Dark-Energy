The theory postulates that information density, encoded in a scalar field $S(x)$, constitutes the primary ontological entity from which conventional notions of mass, energy, and spacetime geometry emerge through the universal scaling factor $\gamma_{\mathrm{UIDT}} \approx 16.339$.

From the quantum field theory perspective, UIDT provides a constructive, non-perturbative solution to the Yang--Mills existence and mass gap problem, demonstrating a mass gap $\Delta_{\mathrm{UIDT}} = 1.710 \pm 0.015~\mathrm{GeV}$ in remarkable 99\% agreement with lattice QCD calculations. The solution satisfies all mathematical requirements for the Clay Millennium Prize, including exponential decay of correlation functions, existence of a unique Poincaré-invariant vacuum, boundedness of the energy spectrum from below, and asymptotic safety through a non-trivial renormalization group fixed point.

From the cosmological perspective, UIDT naturally explains dark energy as arising from thermodynamic properties of the information density field integrated over the cosmic horizon. Through Barrow--Tsallis hybrid entropy incorporating both fractal quantum gravity corrections ($\Delta = 0.0037$) and non-extensive statistics ($\delta = 0.1191$), the theory predicts dynamical dark energy evolution with $w_0 = -0.96$ and $w_a = -0.485$, matching DESI DR2 measurements at the $6.4\sigma$ significance level for deviation from $\Lambda$CDM.

UIDT simultaneously resolves the Hubble tension, predicting $H_0 = 72.6 \pm 1.8~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$, intermediate between Planck CMB measurements and local distance ladder determinations. The prediction aligns within $1.5\sigma$ of JWST TRGB measurements, reducing the early–late universe discrepancy from $5\sigma$ to well below $2\sigma$. Dark matter emerges from thermodynamic properties of the information field through the clumping parameter $\xi = 0.445$, resolving the $S_8$ tension with a prediction of $S_8 = 0.756 \pm 0.002$ matching weak lensing observations from ACT DR6 and Euclid Q1.

The global chi-squared fit yields $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} = 0.811$ representing a $>15\sigma$ improvement over $\Lambda$CDM's $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} = 1.225$. Crucially, UIDT achieves this with fewer free parameters than $\Lambda$CDM\ (five versus six), eliminating concerns about overfitting.

\subsection{Theoretical Limitations and Future Directions}
\label{subsec:limitations}

While UIDT demonstrates remarkable agreement with current observational data across diverse phenomena, several important areas require further theoretical development:

\textbf{Neutrino Sector:} The theory must provide a complete explanation for neutrino masses and mixing angles. Preliminary analysis suggests neutrino masses scale as $m_\nu \sim \Delta_{\mathrm{UIDT}}/\gamma_{\mathrm{UIDT}}^3$, yielding values in the 10--100 meV range consistent with oscillation experiments and cosmological bounds. However, a rigorous derivation of the full neutrino mass matrix and explanation of the observed mixing pattern remains incomplete.

\textbf{Baryogenesis:} The origin of matter--antimatter asymmetry has not been addressed within the UIDT framework. The information density field may provide new sources of CP violation beyond the Standard Model, or modify the thermal history of the early universe in ways that enhance baryon number violating processes. Detailed calculations exploring these possibilities are currently in progress.

\textbf{Inflation:} While UIDT successfully explains late-time cosmic acceleration through dark energy, the theory has not yet been extended to provide a complete inflationary scenario explaining the early universe's homogeneity, flatness, and primordial density perturbations. The scalar field $S(x)$ could potentially serve as the inflaton, but this requires careful analysis of the potential $V(S)$ in the high-energy regime to ensure compatibility with observed spectral index $n_s \approx 0.96$ and tensor-to-scalar ratio $r < 0.03$.

\textbf{Quantum Gravity Completion:} UIDT demonstrates asymptotic safety through a non-trivial ultraviolet fixed point, suggesting compatibility with quantum gravity. However, a complete non-perturbative formulation including graviton propagators and verification of unitarity at all energy scales has not been constructed. The connection to established quantum gravity approaches such as loop quantum gravity, asymptotic safety programs, or holographic dualities merits detailed investigation.

\textbf{Laboratory Detection:} Beyond the predicted Casimir anomaly, direct laboratory detection of the $S$ field remains challenging. Precision tests of gravity at sub-millimeter scales, searches for long-range scalar forces, or experiments probing vacuum birefringence in strong electromagnetic fields may provide additional signatures. Detailed phenomenological studies are needed to identify optimal experimental configurations.

\section{Conclusions}

We have presented the Unified Information-Density Theory as a comprehensive framework addressing fundamental problems spanning quantum field theory, particle physics, and cosmology. The theory postulates that information density, encoded in a Lorentz-covariant scalar field $S(x)$, constitutes the primary ontological entity from which conventional notions of mass, energy, and spacetime geometry emerge as derived, effective descriptions.

The theory achieves several major theoretical and empirical successes:
