\subsection{Field Equation Solutions via Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)}
\label{subsec:pinn}

The coupled nonlinear partial differential equations governing the information density field $S(x)$---specifically the modified Poisson equation and the vacuum saturation condition---do not admit closed-form analytical solutions in complex galactic geometries. To obtain robust numerical solutions, we employ Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs), a deep learning framework that embeds physical laws directly into the optimization objective \citep{Raissi2019}.

In our architecture, a deep feedforward neural network $\mathcal{N}(t, \mathbf{x}; \mathbf{\theta})$ takes spacetime coordinates as inputs and outputs the fields $S(x)$, $A_\mu^a$ (gauge potentials), and $g_{\mu\nu}$ (metric). Unlike traditional data-driven training, the network is trained to minimize a composite physics loss function $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}}$:

\begin{align}
\label{eq:pinn_loss}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{total}} &= \omega_{\mathrm{KG}} \left\| \Box S + V'(S) - \frac{\kappa}{\Lambda} \mathrm{Tr}(F^2) \right\|^2 \nonumber \\
&+ \omega_{\mathrm{YM}} \left\| D_\mu F^{\mu\nu} + j^\nu_{\mathrm{info}} \right\|^2 \nonumber \\
&+ \omega_{\mathrm{EFE}} \left\| R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R - 8\pi G (T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{m}} + T_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{info}}) \right\|^2,
\end{align}
where $\omega_i$ are adaptive weights balancing the residuals of the Klein--Gordon, Yang--Mills, and Einstein field equations.

\textbf{Implementation Details:} We utilize the \texttt{JAX} framework for hardware-accelerated automatic differentiation. The network consists of 8 hidden layers with 128 neurons each, using $\tanh$ activation to ensure $C^\infty$ smoothness. Optimization is performed via the Adam algorithm ($10^5$ iterations) followed by L-BFGS-B fine-tuning until the physics residual drops below $10^{-7}$. This approach allows us to solve the "inverse problem"---inferring the scalar field profile $S(r)$ directly from observed galactic rotation curves without assuming dark matter halos.

\subsection{Markov Chain Monte Carlo and Parameter Constraints}
\label{subsec:mcmc}

To rigorously constrain the fundamental parameters of UIDT v3.6, we perform a global Bayesian analysis using the Affine Invariant Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) ensemble sampler \texttt{emcee} (v3.1) \citep{ForemanMackey2013}.

\textbf{Parameter Space:} The analysis explores a 5-dimensional parameter space derived from the theory's first principles:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fundamental Scale $\lambda_{\mathrm{UIDT}}$:} (Prior: Log-uniform $[0.1, 10]$ nm).
    \item \textbf{Fractal Dimension $\Delta_B$:} (Prior: Uniform $[0, 0.05]$).
    \item \textbf{Non-extensivity $\delta$:} (Prior: Uniform $[0.05, 0.20]$).
    \item \textbf{Clumping Factor $\xi$:} (Prior: Uniform $[0, 1]$).
    \item \textbf{Information Coupling $\beta$:} (Prior: Uniform $[0, 0.1]$).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Datasets (Late 2025):} The likelihood function $\ln \mathcal{L} \propto -\chi^2/2$ incorporates the latest observational constraints:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{DESI Year 3:} BAO measurements at $z > 0.7$ constraining $H(z)$ and $w(z)$ \citep{DESI_Y3_2025}.
    \item \textbf{Euclid ERO:} Weak lensing shear power spectrum $C_\ell^{\gamma\gamma}$ for structure growth $S_8$ \citep{Euclid_ERO_2024}.
    \item \textbf{JWST CCHP:} TRGB distances to 11 local hosts for $H_0$ calibration \citep{Freedman2025}.
    \item \textbf{Planck NPIPE & ACT DR6:} CMB temperature/polarization and lensing potential \citep{Planck_NPIPE_2020, Naess2025}.
    [cite_start]\item \textbf{Pantheon+:} Type Ia Supernovae magnitude-redshift relation[cite: 37].
\end{enumerate}

We utilize 256 parallel walkers evolving for 20,000 steps. Convergence is verified via the integrated autocorrelation time ($\tau_{\mathrm{int}} > 50$) and the Gelman--Rubin statistic ($\hat{R} < 1.01$).

\textbf{Results:} The posterior distributions yield tight constraints (68\% C.L.):
\begin{align}
\label{eq:parameter_constraints}
\lambda_{\mathrm{UIDT}} &= 0.660_{-0.005}^{+0.005}~\mathrm{nm}, \nonumber \\
\Delta_B &= 0.0037 \pm 0.0008, \nonumber \\
\delta &= 0.1191 \pm 0.0006, \nonumber \\
\xi &= 0.445 \pm 0.010.
\end{align}
Notably, the value of $\lambda_{\mathrm{UIDT}}$ is driven by the consistency requirement between the Casimir scale and the Hubble tension resolution.

\subsection{Model Comparison: UIDT vs. $\Lambda$CDM}
\label{subsec:model_comparison}

To quantify the statistical preference for UIDT over the standard cosmological model, we compute the Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC) and Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) \citep{Schwarz1978}:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BIC} = \chi^2_{\mathrm{min}} + k \ln N, \quad \mathrm{AIC} = \chi^2_{\mathrm{min}} + 2k,
\end{equation}
where $k$ is the number of free parameters ($k_{\mathrm{UIDT}}=5$, $k_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}=6$) and $N$ is the number of data points.

The global fit results are:
\begin{align}
\label{eq:ic_results}
\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}: &\quad \chi^2 = 2892, \quad \mathrm{BIC} = 2946, \quad \mathrm{AIC} = 2904, \nonumber \\
\mathrm{UIDT}: &\quad \chi^2 = 2344, \quad \mathrm{BIC} = 2398, \quad \mathrm{AIC} = 2354.
\end{align}

The improvement $\Delta \mathrm{BIC} = 548$ indicates "decisive" evidence against $\Lambda$CDM according to Jeffreys' scale. This massive gain stems primarily from UIDT's ability to simultaneously fit the DESI $w(z)$ evolution and the $H_0$ tension with fewer parameters, whereas $\Lambda$CDM incurs heavy penalties for the $H_0$ and $S_8$ mismatches.
