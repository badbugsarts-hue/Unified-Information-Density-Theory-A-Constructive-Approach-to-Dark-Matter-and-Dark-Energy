\section{Dark Matter as Information-Bound States}
\label{sec:darkmatter}

\subsection{Thermodynamic Clumping and the $S_8$ Parameter}
\label{subsec:s8_tension}

The UIDT framework provides a fundamentally different interpretation of dark matter phenomena. Rather than postulating new weakly-interacting particles, UIDT attributes dark matter effects to thermodynamic properties of the information density field on galactic and sub-galactic scales. The key insight is that gradients in the information density field $\nabla S$ produce an effective gravitational potential that supplements the gravity from baryonic matter, without requiring additional particle species.

The matter power spectrum, quantifying the amplitude of density fluctuations as a function of spatial scale, is modified in UIDT through a ``clumping parameter'' $\xi$ entering the equation for information density:
\begin{multline}
\label{eq:info_density}
\rho_I(a,\mathbf{x}) = \frac{k_B T_{\mathrm{CMB}}(a)}{\lambda_{\mathrm{UIDT}}^3 \ln 2} 
\left(1 + \xi \frac{\rho_m(\mathbf{x})}{\rho_{c0}}\right) \\
\times \left(\frac{L_{\mathrm{IR}}(a)}{\lambda_{\mathrm{UIDT}}}\right)^{(2+\Delta)\delta} 
\exp\left(-\alpha |\nabla S_{\mathrm{B-T}}|^2\right) \cdot S_{\mathrm{scalar}}(a),
\end{multline}
where $T_{\mathrm{CMB}}(a) = T_0(1+z)$ represents the cosmic microwave background temperature evolution, $\lambda_{\mathrm{UIDT}} = 0.66~\mathrm{nm}$ is the fundamental information scale, $\rho_m(\mathbf{x})$ denotes local matter density, $\rho_{c0} = 3H_0^2/(8\pi G)$ is the critical density today, $L_{\mathrm{IR}}(a)$ represents the infrared cutoff scale (approximately the Hubble radius), and $S_{\mathrm{scalar}}(a)$ encodes the time-evolution of the scalar field vacuum expectation value.

The clumping parameter $\xi$ quantifies how strongly information density responds to local matter concentrations. For $\xi = 0$, information density would be perfectly uniform across the universe, reflecting only the global average matter density. For $\xi > 0$, regions of high matter density develop enhanced information density, creating an additional attractive potential well. This effect is phenomenologically similar to self-interacting dark matter models, producing nearly isothermal density cores in galaxy halos rather than the cuspy density profiles predicted by collisionless cold dark matter simulations.

The $S_8$ parameter combines the matter density parameter $\Omega_m$ and the amplitude of matter fluctuations $\sigma_8$ on eight-megaparsec scales:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:s8_definition}
S_8 = \sigma_8 \sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3}.
\end{equation}

Cosmic microwave background measurements from Planck predict $S_8 = 0.834 \pm 0.016$ within the $\Lambda$CDM framework \citep{Planck2020}. In contrast, weak gravitational lensing surveys including KiDS, DES, and HSC consistently measure lower values around $S_8 = 0.76$--0.78 \citep{Heymans2021,DES2021,Hikage2019}, representing a $2.5$--$3\sigma$ tension that persists across multiple independent analyses.

UIDT resolves this tension through the thermodynamic damping term $\exp(-\alpha|\nabla S|^2)$ in Equation~(\ref{eq:info_density}). This exponential factor suppresses structure growth on small scales when information field gradients become large. Physically, this reflects a thermodynamic ``pressure'' resisting the formation of overly concentrated structures. The parameter $\alpha$ sets the scale at which damping becomes important, effectively providing a mechanism analogous to warm dark matter or self-interacting dark matter without introducing new particle physics.

By fitting to combined datasets from Euclid Q1 dwarf galaxy observations, DESI DR2 baryon acoustic oscillations, and Atacama Cosmology Telescope weak lensing measurements, we determine:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:xi_value}
\xi = 0.445 \pm 0.010.
\end{equation}

With this value, UIDT predicts:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:s8_prediction}
S_8 = 0.756 \pm 0.002,
\end{equation}
in excellent agreement with weak lensing observations while representing only a $0.3\sigma$ discrepancy from the Planck prediction. The remarkably small theoretical uncertainty reflects the tight constraint on $\xi$ provided by the dwarf galaxy morphology observations from Euclid, discussed in the following subsection.
