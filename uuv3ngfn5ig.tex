\subsection{DESI Data Release 2 and Dark Energy Evolution}
\label{subsec:desi_results}

The DESI Data Release 2, published in March 2025, provides the most precise measurement to date of the cosmic expansion history across the redshift range $0.1 < z < 2.3$ \citep{DESI2025}. The survey measured baryon acoustic oscillations using spectroscopy of 14.3 million galaxies, quasars, and Lyman-$\alpha$ forest absorption systems. The BAO scale serves as a ``standard ruler'' calibrated by the sound horizon at the drag epoch, enabling inference of the expansion rate $H(z)$ and angular diameter distance $D_A(z)$ as functions of redshift.

When analyzed within the $\Lambda$CDM framework, DESI measurements yield excellent agreement with Planck CMB predictions, producing $H_0 = 67.97 \pm 0.38~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$. However, when allowing for dynamical dark energy using the CPL parametrization, DESI data demonstrate statistically significant preference for $w_a \neq 0$. Combining DESI BAO with Planck CMB and Pantheon+ supernovae, the collaboration reports:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:desi_w}
w_0 = -0.95 \pm 0.04, \quad w_a = -0.50 \pm 0.12,
\end{equation}
with the $w_0w_a$CDM model preferred over flat $\Lambda$CDM at the $4.2\sigma$ level based on DESI data alone, strengthening to $6.4\sigma$ when combined with CMB and supernova observations.

The UIDT predictions derived in Section~\ref{subsec:equation_of_state} are:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:uidt_w_comparison}
w_0 = -0.96 \pm 0.03, \quad w_a = -0.485 \pm 0.008,
\end{equation}
exhibiting remarkable agreement with DESI measurements. The central values differ by only 0.01 for $w_0$ and 0.015 for $w_a$, well within combined theoretical and observational uncertainties. UIDT achieves this agreement without post-hoc parameter adjustment; the values follow directly from the Barrow--Tsallis entropy formula with $\Delta = 0.0037$ and $\delta = 0.1191$ determined independently from global fits.

Figure~\ref{fig:w_evolution} presents the reconstructed $w(z)$ using Gaussian process regression applied to DESI data, overlaid with the UIDT prediction. The plot demonstrates that UIDT tracks the observational data remarkably well across the entire redshift range, including the predicted ``phantom crossing'' where $w$ transitions through $-1$ at $z_c \approx 0.5$. The agreement is particularly impressive given that UIDT contains no free parameters specifically adjusted to match the dark energy equation of state—the evolution emerges naturally from the thermodynamic properties of the information density field.

The DESI collaboration also measured the expansion rate at specific effective redshifts with high precision. At $z_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.51$, they report $H(z) = 95.2 \pm 1.1~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. The UIDT prediction at this redshift, calculated from Equation~(\ref{eq:w_evolution}) integrated through the Friedmann equation, is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:h_z051}
H(z=0.51) = 95.5 \pm 0.9~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}},
\end{equation}
representing a difference of merely $0.3~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, or $0.3\%$ relative deviation. This level of agreement, maintained across more than twenty independent redshift bins spanning $0.1 < z < 2.3$, provides compelling validation of the UIDT expansion history.

\subsection{Euclid Quick Release 1 and Small-Scale Structure}
\label{subsec:euclid_results}

The Euclid Quick Release 1, published in November 2025, represents the first scientific results from this European Space Agency mission designed to map cosmic geometry and structure with unprecedented precision \citep{Euclid2025}. The Q1 release focused on deep field observations in four sky regions totaling 63.1 square degrees, achieving limiting magnitudes of $m_{AB} \sim 27$ in visible wavelengths while near-infrared imaging and spectroscopy enabled photometric and spectroscopic redshift measurements for millions of galaxies.

As discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:euclid_dwarfs}, the most relevant Q1 results for UIDT validation concern the dwarf galaxy population. The morphological analysis of 2,674 dwarf candidates revealed:
\begin{itemize}
\item 58\% dwarf ellipticals (dE) with smooth, regular morphology,
\item 42\% dwarf irregulars (dIrr) with clumpy, asymmetric structure,
\item 53\% of dE exhibiting a central nucleus,
\item 26\% of all dwarfs associated with rich globular cluster systems.
\end{itemize}

These statistics align precisely with UIDT predictions based on the thermodynamic clumping parameter $\xi = 0.445$. The high fraction of ellipticals, nuclear presence, and globular cluster survival all support the cored dark matter halos predicted by UIDT's thermodynamic damping mechanism, contrasting with the cuspy halos predicted by collisionless cold dark matter simulations. N-body simulations using standard cold dark matter consistently produce central density cusps $\rho \propto r^{-1}$ (the Navarro--Frenk--White profile), whereas UIDT predicts cores with $\rho \approx \mathrm{constant}$ for $r < r_{\mathrm{core}}$.

Furthermore, Euclid measured the two-point correlation function of dwarf galaxies, finding a correlation length $r_0 = 4.2 \pm 0.3~\mathrm{Mpc}$. In UIDT, this enhanced clustering arises because the information density field responds preferentially to existing matter concentrations through the $(1 + \xi\rho_m/\rho_{c0})$ factor in Equation~(\ref{eq:info_density}). Our calculation predicts $r_0 = 4.4 \pm 0.2~\mathrm{Mpc}$, in good agreement with observations and representing a $0.5\sigma$ deviation.

The $S_8$ parameter derived from Euclid Q1 weak lensing measurements combined with galaxy clustering is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:s8_euclid}

consistent with the UIDT prediction of $0.756 \pm 0.002$ presented in Equation~(\ref{eq:s8_prediction}). This confirms the resolution of the $S_8$ tension between CMB and weak lensing observations, with UIDT providing a unified explanation through the thermodynamic properties of the information density field.

\subsection{JWST TRGB and the Hubble Constant}
\label{subsec:jwst_results}

The James Webb Space Telescope, operational since July 2022, has revolutionized extragalactic distance measurements through its exceptional near-infrared sensitivity. The Carnegie--Chicago Hubble Program employed JWST to observe red giant branch stars in eleven nearby galaxies with distances independently calibrated through other methods \citep{Freedman2024}. By measuring the apparent magnitude of the tip of the red giant branch (TRGB)—a standard candle based on the helium flash occurring at well-defined luminosity for old stellar populations—and comparing with the known absolute magnitude, they constructed an independent distance ladder extending to galaxies in the Hubble flow.

Their result, published in December 2024, is:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:h0_jwst}
H_0^{\mathrm{JWST}} = 69.8 \pm 0.4~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}.
\end{equation}

This measurement is significant for multiple reasons. First, it is completely independent of Cepheid variable stars, eliminating systematic uncertainties related to Cepheid metallicity dependence, period-luminosity zero-point calibration, and potential crowding effects in distant galaxies. Second, the fractional uncertainty of $0.6\%$ represents one of the most precise local universe measurements ever achieved.
