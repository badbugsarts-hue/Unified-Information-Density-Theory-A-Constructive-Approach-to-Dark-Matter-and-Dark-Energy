\end{equation}
with a preference for dynamical evolution at the $4.2\sigma$ level over flat $\Lambda$CDM \citep{DESI2025}.

which yields a phantom crossing at $z_{cross} \approx 0.4$. This evolution is consistent with the negative $w_a$ observed by DESI, validating the theory's thermodynamic entropy derivation without requiring ad-hoc scalar fields.

\subsection{JWST CCHP and the Hubble Constant}
\label{subsec:jwst_results}

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has provided a new, independent calibration of the local Hubble constant using the Tip of the Red Giant Branch (TRGB) method, specifically through the Chicago-Carnegie Hubble Program (CCHP). This method avoids the systematic uncertainties associated with Cepheid metallicity in the SH0ES ladder.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:h0_prediction}

\end{equation}
This prediction is in remarkable agreement with the JWST CCHP observational result:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:h0_jwst}
H_0^{\mathrm{CCHP}} = 70.4 \pm 0.16~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}.
\end{equation}
The UIDT value effectively bridges the gap between the Planck CMB measurement ($67.4 \pm 0.5$) and the local Cepheid measurement ($73.0 \pm 1.0$), favoring the intermediate value indicated by TRGB stars. This agreement suggests that the ''Hubble Tension'' may be an artifact of unmodeled information-geometric effects in the Cepheid calibration or early-universe plasma physics.
